What is a Message Queue?

  • It allows different parts of a system to communicate asynchronously;
  • It is nothing but a queue of messages, with one/more producers and one/more consumers;
  • Messages are stored in the queue until it is processed; Each message is processed only once; Once processed, it is deleted;
  • For the above reason, it is called point-to-point communication;
  • It is useful in scenarios where you want: to decouple heavyweight processing, to buffer and process as a batch, to smoothen spiky workloads;

  • JMS is the specification; Implementations: Apache ActiveMQ, RabbitMQ;

What is a Streaming?

  • This is also called publisher-subscriber messaging;
  • Messages are organized into topics; There could be many topics;
  • Each consumer can subscribe to one or more topics, in which he is interested in;
  • A Producer can produce messages of any topic type and pubish it;
  • When producer publishes a message to a topic, unlike messaging, message is pushed to all subscribers of that topic, with little or no queuing;
  • This is also called Event-driven Architecture - Pubisher need not to know, who the consumer is and Consumer need not to know, who the producer is;

  • Implementations: Apache Kafka, Amazon Kinesis;

Messaging VS Streaming

  • In case of messaging, a single message is the unit of work; Consumer can understand each message in isolation and can directly act upon it;
  • In case of streaming, a signle message doesn't make much sense; You need to aggregate messages over a period of time or based on the count, to make a sense of it;

  • Messaging need not to be a continous flow;
  • Stream is a constant flow of messages;

  • In case of messaging, a message is deleted, once it is consumed;
  • In case of streaming, messages stay in the system for certain amount of time, whether they are processed or not;

  • Messaging system doesn't support querying and analytics;
  • You could query and run analytics on the stream itself;

  • In case of messaging, no need to track the position of the message, as each message is processed independantly and is deleted after processing;
  • In case of streaming, consumer or some other process needs to track till what point consumer processed the messages;

When to use what?

  • Messaging and streaming are complementary technologies rather than competing technologies; They serve different purposes in the messaging industry;
  • Deciding which technology to pick should be based on the use case;
