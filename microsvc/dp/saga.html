1. What is the Saga Pattern?
  • A Saga is a sequence of local transactions where each transaction updates data within a single service;
  • If a local transaction fails, the Saga executes a series of compensating transactions to undo the changes made by the preceding local transactions;
  • It is used to manage distributed transactions and maintain data consistency across microservices;
2. Types of Sagas
  • Choreography-based Saga:
    • Each service involved in the saga produces and listens to events;
    • There is no central coordinator; services react to events from other services;
  • Orchestration-based Saga:
    • A central orchestrator (coordinator) tells the participants what local transactions to execute;
    • The orchestrator handles the flow and decides when to trigger compensating transactions;
3. Benefits and Drawbacks
  • Benefits: Enables long-running transactions across multiple services without locking resources;
  • Drawbacks: Increased complexity in implementation and testing; requires careful design of compensating transactions;
