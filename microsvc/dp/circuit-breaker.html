1. What is the Circuit Breaker Pattern?
  • The Circuit Breaker pattern prevents a service from repeatedly trying to execute an operation that is likely to fail;
  • It acts as a proxy that monitors for failures and "trips" when a threshold is reached, blocking further calls;
  • This allows the failing service time to recover and prevents cascading failures;
2. States of a Circuit Breaker
  • Closed: The circuit is closed, and requests are allowed to pass through. Failures are tracked;
  • Open: The threshold of failures is reached. Requests are immediately failed (short-circuited) without calling the service;
  • Half-Open: After a timeout, the circuit allows a limited number of test requests to see if the service has recovered;
3. Benefits
  • Resilience: Protects the system from cascading failures;
  • Graceful Degradation: Allows providing fallback responses when a service is down;
  • Faster Recovery: Prevents overloading a struggling service with more requests;
