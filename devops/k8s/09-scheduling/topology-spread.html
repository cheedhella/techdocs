Topology Spread Constraints

1. What are Topology Spread Constraints?
  • Control how Pods are distributed across failure domains (nodes, zones, regions);
  • More flexible than Pod Anti-Affinity for achieving even distribution;
  • Useful for high availability — ensure Pods are spread across zones;
2. Example
spec:
  topologySpreadConstraints:
    - maxSkew: 1
      topologyKey: topology.kubernetes.io/zone
      whenUnsatisfiable: DoNotSchedule
      labelSelector:
        matchLabels:
          app: myapp
3. Key Fields
FieldDescription
maxSkewMax difference in Pod count between any two topology domains
topologyKeyNode label key that defines the topology domain
whenUnsatisfiableDoNotSchedule (hard) or ScheduleAnyway (soft)
labelSelectorWhich Pods to consider when counting
4. Practical Example — Zone Spreading
# With 3 replicas across 3 zones, maxSkew: 1 ensures:
# Zone A: 1 Pod
# Zone B: 1 Pod
# Zone C: 1 Pod
#
# If Zone C is full, maxSkew: 1 means the max difference
# between any two zones is 1:
# Zone A: 2 Pods, Zone B: 1 Pod — allowed (skew = 1)
# Zone A: 3 Pods, Zone B: 1 Pod — NOT allowed (skew = 2)
5. Multiple Constraints
spec:
  topologySpreadConstraints:
    - maxSkew: 1
      topologyKey: topology.kubernetes.io/zone
      whenUnsatisfiable: DoNotSchedule
      labelSelector:
        matchLabels:
          app: myapp
    - maxSkew: 1
      topologyKey: kubernetes.io/hostname
      whenUnsatisfiable: ScheduleAnyway
      labelSelector:
        matchLabels:
          app: myapp

This spreads Pods evenly across zones (hard) and across nodes (soft).
