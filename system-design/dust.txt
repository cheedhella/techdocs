  Distributed News Feed System Design

---
When you open LinkedIn and your feed loads in under a second, we are seeing one of the hardest problems in distributed systems — assembling a personalised, ranked feed within 100 milliseconds for one billion simultaneous users.

---
**The Fan-out Problem**
A celebrity with 500M followers publishing one post generates 500M Cassandra writes under a naive model, saturating the storage cluster for over 8 minutes. The hybrid fan-out model solves this. Regular users receive write-time fan-out, pre-populating feed caches for instant reads. Accounts above a dynamically adjustable threshold receive read-time fan-out, storing a single event record. The threshold adjusts in real time based on load with no deployment required.

**The Ranking Problem**
A personalised ranked feed within 100ms requires two pre-computed signals: relationship strength scores from months of interaction history with time-decay weighting, and engagement velocity scores reflecting current momentum. The ranking computation (300-500ms) never runs in the reader's critical path — it runs proactively before any request is made.

**The Stack**
Kafka (4 topics), Cassandra (5 purpose-built tables), Redis (4 key spaces — ranked feed 15min TTL, post content 1hr, relationship strength 6hr, engagement velocity 1hr). Spring Boot with WebFlux, Spring Data Cassandra, Spring Data Redis with Lettuce, Spring Kafka, Spring Cloud Config, ShedLock, and Micrometer with OpenTelemetry.

**Back-of-Envelope Numbers**
5,780 posts per second average, 58K at peak. Celebrity fan-out reduced from 500M Cassandra writes to 1 write per post. 47GB Redis working memory at 100M active users. 54TB Cassandra storage across both tables with replication factor 3. 14-20 node Cassandra cluster. Single Ranking Engine instance at 150 computations per second is sufficient for steady-state 100M active users on 15-minute TTL cycles.

**The Trade-offs**
The 15-minute TTL means a reader may see content up to 15 minutes old — acceptable for social engagement, not for financial systems. The significance threshold creates asymmetric timing: strong connections get near-real-time updates, weak connections wait for TTL expiry. Deliberate product decision.

**What the Diagram Does Not Show**
Four production-essential components omitted due to space but fully expandable: Redis primary-replica clusters with Sentinel failover, Cassandra multi-node replication with LOCAL_QUORUM consistency, Kafka per-topic dead letter queues with exponential backoff, and the proactive WebSocket path where FeedUpdateWorker publishes through Redis pub/sub to notify connected readers of new content without disrupting their scroll.

🚩 Interview Trap: "We'll just add more replicas."
Read replicas solve read scaling.
But they don't solve write scaling.
At all.

𝐄𝐯𝐞𝐫𝐲 𝐫𝐞𝐩𝐥𝐢𝐜𝐚 𝐬𝐭𝐢𝐥𝐥 𝐫𝐞𝐜𝐞𝐢𝐯𝐞𝐬 𝐞𝐯𝐞𝐫𝐲 𝐰𝐫𝐢𝐭𝐞 𝐟𝐫𝐨𝐦 𝐭𝐡𝐞 𝐩𝐫𝐢𝐦𝐚𝐫𝐲.
10 replicas means 10 copies of every write.
The write load on your primary stays exactly the same.

If your bottleneck is writes, replicas won't help.
You need sharding.
If your bottleneck is reads, replicas are perfect.

Confusing these two is one of the most common mistakes in system design interviews.
And interviewers catch it instantly.
